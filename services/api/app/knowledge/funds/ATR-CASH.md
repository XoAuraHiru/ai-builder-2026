# Atrium Cash Reserve (ATR-CASH)

## Objective

Preserve capital and earn short-term Treasury yields with daily liquidity.

## What it holds

Atrium Cash Reserve holds 2 exchange-traded funds in fixed proportions, rebalanced monthly.

| ETF | Weight | Asset class | Region | ETF expense ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SGOV | 60% | Cash | US | 0.09% |
| BIL | 40% | Cash | US | 0.14% |

## Who it suits

Emergency funds, parking cash between investments, horizons under one year.

It is risk band 1 of 5 (Preserve). It suits investors whose risk profile is band 1 or higher.

## Fees

Atrium fee: 0.10% a year, which is $10 on every $10,000 invested.

Underlying ETF costs: about 0.11% a year, from each ETF's published expense ratio. They are already reflected in the ETF prices.

Total yearly cost: about 0.21%, or $21 on $10,000. There are no entry, exit, switching or performance fees.

## How it behaved

From July 2007 to September 2026, the fund's mix returned 1.4% a year on average before the Atrium fee, with yearly swings (volatility) of about 0.6%.

Its best calendar year was 2024 (+5.2%) and its worst was 2013 (-0.1%).

Its largest fall from a peak was 0.4%, from October 2009 to October 2015.

## Risks

Treasury-bill ETFs are among the steadiest investments, but their prices can still dip when short-term interest rates move, and their yield can trail inflation.

Unlike a bank deposit, this fund is not insured by the FDIC.

## Important information

The fund launched on October 1, 2024. Figures before then are a backtest of its target mix using the ETFs' real month-end prices. The backtest starts in July 2007. SGOV has prices from June 2020; before that, its 60% weight is spread across the fund's other ETFs.

Backtests are hypothetical. Past performance does not predict future returns, and you can lose money. Atrium is a prototype with synthetic accounts and paper trading; nothing here is investment advice.
