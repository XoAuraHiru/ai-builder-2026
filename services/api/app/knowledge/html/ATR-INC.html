Atrium Core Income (ATR-INC)

Objective

Steady income from a diversified portfolio of US investment-grade bonds.

What it holds

Atrium Core Income holds 3 exchange-traded funds in fixed proportions, rebalanced monthly.

Who it suits

Income seekers and investors with horizons of one to three years.

It is risk band 2 of 5 (Cautious). It suits investors whose risk profile is band 2 or higher.

Fees

Atrium fee: 0.25% a year, which is $25 on every $10,000 invested.

Underlying ETF costs: about 0.03% a year, from each ETF's published expense ratio. They are already reflected in the ETF prices.

Total yearly cost: about 0.28%, or $28 on $10,000. There are no entry, exit, switching or performance fees.

How it behaved

From July 2007 to September 2026, the fund's mix returned 3.2% a year on average before the Atrium fee, with yearly swings (volatility) of about 4.8%.

Its best calendar year was 2019 (+9.8%) and its worst was 2022 (-13.3%).

Its largest fall from a peak was 17.4%, from December 2020 to October 2022.

Risks

Bond prices fall when interest rates rise, so this fund can lose value, as it did when rates rose quickly in 2022.

Corporate bonds add credit risk: a company can fail to pay what it owes.

Important information

The fund launched on October 1, 2024. Figures before then are a backtest of its target mix using the ETFs' real month-end prices. The backtest starts in July 2007. VCIT has prices from November 2009; before that, its 20% weight is spread across the fund's other ETFs.

Backtests are hypothetical. Past performance does not predict future returns, and you can lose money. Atrium is a prototype with synthetic accounts and paper trading; nothing here is investment advice.
