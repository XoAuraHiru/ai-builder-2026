Atrium Dividend (ATR-DIV)

Objective

Growing income and moderate growth from quality US dividend payers.

What it holds

Atrium Dividend holds 2 exchange-traded funds in fixed proportions, rebalanced monthly. Its largest underlying companies are Merck & Co Inc (3.1%), Chevron Corp (3.1%), Texas Instruments Inc (2.8%), Broadcom Inc (2.8%), Qualcomm Inc (2.7%).

Who it suits

Investors who want income with equity growth and medium to long horizons.

It is risk band 3 of 5 (Balanced). It suits investors whose risk profile is band 3 or higher.

Fees

Atrium fee: 0.35% a year, which is $35 on every $10,000 invested.

Underlying ETF costs: about 0.05% a year, from each ETF's published expense ratio. They are already reflected in the ETF prices.

Total yearly cost: about 0.40%, or $40 on $10,000. There are no entry, exit, switching or performance fees.

How it behaved

From July 2007 to September 2026, the fund's mix returned 9.3% a year on average before the Atrium fee, with yearly swings (volatility) of about 14.9%.

Its best calendar year was 2013 (+31.8%) and its worst was 2008 (-31.9%).

Its largest fall from a peak was 51.8%, from September 2007 to February 2009.

Risks

Companies can cut their dividends, and dividend stocks can lag the market when fast-growing companies lead.

This is a stock fund: it can fall sharply in a crash despite its income focus.

Important information

The fund launched on October 1, 2024. Figures before then are a backtest of its target mix using the ETFs' real month-end prices. The backtest starts in July 2007. SCHD has prices from October 2011; before that, its 60% weight is spread across the fund's other ETFs.

Backtests are hypothetical. Past performance does not predict future returns, and you can lose money. Atrium is a prototype with synthetic accounts and paper trading; nothing here is investment advice.
